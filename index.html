A signal is a route card from someone who walked the mountain before you.

A hiking guide doesn't carry you up the hill. They give you a card: start here, rest at these viewpoints, and if the weather turns at this rock — go back down. You still walk it yourself.

A CAIRN signal is that card for one trade. Five lines, always in the same order, always with a turn-back point. Below, every line is explained with an example from ordinary life.

How to read a signal — in everyday words

  3. Targets65 900 · 67 300 · 68 900
  4. Stop-loss62 900
  5. Leverage× 3 max
  6. Risk per trade1 %

  1. Pair and direction — your shopping list

    “BTC/USDT · LONG” reads as: buy bitcoin with dollars, because we expect it to get more expensive. “SHORT” is the reverse — you earn if the price goes down. Like a shopping list: it says what to buy, not when to panic.

  2. Entry zone — buy on the discount shelf

    We give a price range, not one magic number. Think of a jacket on sale between Monday and Wednesday: anywhere inside that window is a fair price. If the price already ran away above the zone — don't chase it, the sale is over.

  3. Targets — sell the harvest in parts

    A gardener doesn't wait for one perfect day to sell every apple. A third goes on Monday, a third midweek, the last third if prices stay good. At each target (TP1, TP2, TP3) you close a part of the trade and put profit in your pocket.

  4. Stop-loss — the fuse in your flat

    When something shorts, the fuse cuts the power before the wiring burns. The stop-loss closes the trade at a small, known loss before it turns into a big one. Some trades will hit it — that is the fuse working, not failing. Never trade without it.

  5. After the first target — a free ticket home

    Once TP1 is reached we post “move stop to entry”. Like a return ticket that's already paid for: the rest of the trip can no longer cost you your own money (apart from small fees and slippage).

  6. Leverage — a rented truck

    A truck carries more than a car, but every pothole hits harder. With ×3 leverage a 1 % move in price becomes 3 % of your money — both ways. We always write the maximum; beginners can ignore it and trade without leverage at all.

  7. Risk per trade — don't put the salary on one horse

    Size each trade so that hitting the stop costs about 1–2 % of your deposit. Then even five losses in a row are a scratch, not a disaster — and you're still there for the good trades.

Honest answers

Can I lose money?

Yes. Some signals end at the stop-loss — you can see them in the journal. That's why every signal has a stop and why we recommend risking only 1–2 % of your deposit per trade.

Do I need to understand charts?

No. You need to copy five numbers into an order form. Our guide shows where each one goes on popular exchanges.

How much money do I need to start?

Most exchanges allow trades from about $10. Start with an amount you are fully prepared to lose while you learn the routine.

What if I missed the entry?

If the price is already above the entry zone (for a long) — skip the trade. Chasing a price is the most common beginner mistake. Another route will come.

Is this financial advice?

No. Signals are analytical information and education. You decide whether to trade and you are responsible for your account.
